# 2026-08-17

## An ink-and-flat-colour resolve pass, generalized out of one game and proved against another

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-17

**Action:** Added `StylizedResolvePass` and its tone-banding maths to `voxel/three`, wired it into `ThreeRenderRuntime` as an opt-in option plus a live setter, and exported the surface. `aoe2` is the first consumer.

### Where it came from

The look already existed in the sibling `townscaper` repo, as `src/rendering/artStyleResolvePass.ts`. Two facts decided that it belonged here rather than being copied a third time.

It is scene-agnostic. The pass reads depth, view-space normals and luminance. Nothing in it knows what a building or a unit is, so nothing in it is specific to the game that grew it.

`ThreeRenderRuntime` has exactly one `renderer.render(this.scene, this.camera)` call, at what was `ThreeRenderRuntime.ts:991`. Every frame path routes through `renderCurrent()`, and so does the capture path via `runtimeCaptureSupport.ts`. Hooking there means a stylized capture cannot disagree with the canvas it claims to show — which is also what made the aoe2 pixel-diff verification trustworthy.

Both engines are orthographic. The depth gradient assumes a linear depth buffer; under a perspective projection the same world step reads as a vanishing depth difference at distance and the contour would thin toward the horizon and stop. `voxel` is `createIsometricOrthographicCamera` throughout, so the maths ported unchanged.

### What deliberately did not port

`townscaper` bands its sea separately, over a measured `WATER_TONE_RANGE` of 0.55..0.88, and reaches it in the shader through `step(d, 0.9999)` — pixels where nothing wrote depth. That works because its water is drawn with `depthWrite` off. It is one game's measurement of one game's water, and shipping it as a library default would have published it as a property of the technique.

It ships as a generic **background lane** instead: `backgroundBands`, `backgroundToneRange`, `backgroundToneSoftness`, each defaulting to the scene lane so the split is a no-op until a game measures its own. `GRADIENT_TONE_SOFTNESS` (0.22) is exported with its full provenance rather than applied as a default, because a hard staircase is what "flat bands" means and softness is a remedy for one specific artefact. `tests/three/stylized-resolve-pass.test.ts` pins this: *makes the background lane a no-op until a game measures its own*.

### What the port improved

`townscaper` denominates its depth threshold in raw depth-buffer difference (`depthEdgeScale: 900`), which is meaningless outside its own near and far planes. Here the pass reads the camera's depth range each frame and converts to world units, so `depthEdgeScale` means "ink per world unit of step" and survives a change of rig. Pinned by *reads the depth range from an orthographic camera so the threshold is in world units*.

The scene-state restore moved into a `finally`. Without it a single failed frame leaves `scene.overrideMaterial` set to the normal material and a camera layer switched off — a corruption that outlives the frame that caused it and reads as a broken scene rather than a failed pass. **Confirmed to bite:** neutralizing the `try`/`finally` into two bare blocks made *restores the caller scene and camera even when the normal render throws* fail at the `scene.overrideMaterial` assertion, and it passed again when restored.

### What a reviewer would have caught, found here instead

`RendererLike` deliberately describes only what drawing a frame needs, and a host may satisfy it with a non-`WebGLRenderer` adapter. The pass needs `getRenderTarget`, `setRenderTarget` and `shadowMap`. Rather than failing as an undefined call mid-frame, `supportsStylizedResolve` refuses at wire-up with a message naming all three members, the renderer that satisfies them, and the option to omit. Pinned by *refuses a renderer that cannot redirect its output, and says what would*.

The pass is sized in **device** pixels, not CSS pixels. A target sized in CSS pixels resamples the frame on a high-DPI display and softens every contour the pass exists to draw. Pinned by *sizes the pass in device pixels, at construction and across a resize*.

### Numbers the first consumer moved

Measured in `aoe2` on its default view (`aoe2-prototype`, 800x600), as mean colour over the world canvas. Painted baseline L=90.1.

| Configuration | L | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Painted (no pass) | 90.1 | baseline |
| Pass-through (`flatten: 0, inkStrength: 0`) | 90.2 | colour pipeline is faithful |
| Banding only (`inkStrength: 0`) | 93.8 | banding *brightens* |
| Preset as shipped (`normalEdgeScale: 2.4`) | 69.8 | 23% darker — unusable |
| `normalEdgeScale: 0.25` | 88.4 | haze over blocks, not line |
| Silhouettes only, `depthEdgeScale: 1.2` | 86.8 | clean, but muted |
| `depthEdgeScale: 2.4` | 82.1 | units blob |
| Tuned, `saturation: 1.4, brightness: 1.12` | 96.9 | the values chosen |
| **Shipped**, after the review's ink colour-space fix | **100.8** | same values, correct ink |

The pass-through row is the one that mattered for diagnosis. The frame went visibly dark and green on first run, and the obvious suspect was the linear/sRGB round trip through the render target. Setting `flatten: 0, inkStrength: 0` reproduced the baseline to within 0.1 luminance, which ruled the colour pipeline out in one measurement and turned the whole problem into tuning.

### A finding worth carrying

**A normal-crease edge source cannot discriminate in voxel geometry.** In smooth geometry a normal break is a crease inside one form — the thing that draws a roof ridge against the wall beneath it. In voxel geometry every face junction is exactly 90 degrees, so the turn between neighbouring normals is exactly 1.0 at *every* cube edge, and any `normalEdgeScale` at or above 1 inks all of them equally. It is not a weak signal, it is a constant one. This is why the shipped preset cost a quarter of the frame's brightness on a voxel scene and none on the smooth-geometry game it was tuned for.

### What the review caught that the author missed

Escalated to multi-CLI review as a sibling-repo change. **Codex was unreachable** — usage limit until Aug 19 — so the count was made up with a second Claude instance carrying Codex's GPU-lifecycle lens. This change has had no independent second-engine read; retry next session. Full synthesis in `aoe2/docs/threads/current/moebius-art-style/2026-08-17/1/REVIEW.md`.

Seventeen findings, all acted on. The ones that mattered:

- **The bound render target was not restored on a throw.** The `finally` protected `overrideMaterial`, the layer mask, and `shadowMap.needsUpdate` — the three states the comment enumerated — and missed the fourth piece of caller state the pass mutates. On a borrowed renderer, one failed frame would leave an offscreen target bound and every later host draw would land invisibly in a buffer the pass later deallocates. Both scene renders are inside the `try` now. Confirmed by neutralizing the restore and watching two tests go red.
- **The metrics collapsed to the resolve quad.** Three resets `renderer.info` per render, so a three-render frame reported one draw call and two triangles whatever the scene held. No gate caught it, because the browser specs assert only `drawCalls > 0`.
- **The shadow-map suppression suppressed nothing.** Three early-outs only when `autoUpdate` *and* `needsUpdate` are false. The save/restore of `needsUpdate` alone sat there looking like it had prevented the double shadow render it did not prevent.
- **The ink was blended in the wrong colour space.** Three converts an authored sRGB colour to linear on upload; the frame is display-encoded by the time the ink mixes in. `#2b3a45` was drawing at roughly `#0a0e12`. The near-black default hid it completely — the aoe2 look was tuned against an accidentally darker line, and the first light-ink style would have exposed it. Correcting the encode moved the shipped frame from L=96.9 to L=100.8.
- **A comment claimed a gate that does not exist.** `stylizedToneBands.ts` said body divergence "is caught by the browser gate"; the devlog above correctly listed that gate as the missing half. The source was asserting as done what the history recorded as outstanding — and it is precisely the gap the `floor(bands)` divergence slipped through. Now stated plainly.
- **A test passed with its fix neutralized.** The refused-swap test asserted the *identity* of the stored pass, which cannot tell "incumbent alive" from "incumbent disposed" — exactly the state a release-then-build ordering leaves. It renders a frame after the refusal now.
- **Two options were accepted where they do nothing.** An embedded host never routes its draw through this runtime's seam, and a perspective camera silently degrades the contour. Both are refused at wire-up now, matching the refusal the unsupported-renderer path already had.

### Follow-ups

- The GLSL and TypeScript halves of the banding maths are hand-transliterated, and no node gate executes the GLSL. `TONE_BAND_UNIFORM_NAMES` pins the *interface* on both sides; divergence in the function **bodies** is currently unguarded. A browser-gate test rendering a known ramp through the real shader and comparing against `steppedLuminance` is the missing half, and the comment in `stylizedToneBands.ts` says so.
- Tuning was verified at the default camera zoom only. The threshold is world-space so it does not drift with zoom, but line *density* relative to content does, and no capture exercised a second zoom.
