# Devlog — 2026-08-14

Opened on three owner observations, not a plan: Machine works has no simulation on/off toggle, Riverfall's simulation appears to do nothing, and in the chain link study a left click should grab rather than turn the camera. The first two turned out to have the same cause on different sides of one seam, and the third generalised into a single pointer rule when the owner asked for the control logic to be unified everywhere.

## Two switches for one question is how a scene ends up with none

The stage's `simulation on/off` control was wired to `StudioSceneAnimationTransport`, which owns exactly one thing: the scene clock that drives authored recipe motion, moving point lights, and pose replays. Its visibility came from `SceneSessionV1.hasMotion()`, which is `poseReplay !== null || movingLights > 0 || animatedInstances > 0`.

Machine works satisfies none of those. Every recipe in `machine-works-recipes.ts` declares `periodMs: 0`, the scene carries no lights, and it stopped being a replay scene when every scene went live. So the control correctly concluded the scene could not move, and hid itself — on the one scene in the catalog whose entire content is simulation.

The live solver had a pause, but only in the physics-playground panel, which `sceneOpened` shows only for scene ids in `createPhysicsPlaygroundStationsV1()`. Machine works, Riverfall, the windmill, and the chain therefore had no way to stop at all. Riverfall had the opposite failure: it owns two animated recipes (foam at 3,000 ms, kelp at 5,600 ms), so it showed the switch — and turning it off stopped the kelp swaying while the water kept running, because the solver was on the other side of the seam.

`StudioLiveInteract.setRunning` is the missing half. `sceneCanSimulate()` in `studio-app.ts` is the union — authored motion, a moving light, or a live-physics profile — and it now drives the control's visibility, the status suffix, the stage hint, and the `Space` shortcut. `sceneHasMotion()` on the harness was deliberately left meaning authored motion: `model-studio-navigation.spec.ts` uses it to find a scene with a *timeline* to scrub, and a live scene has none.

## A pointer whose meaning depends on what it hit

`pointerdown` had four separate left-button fallbacks to `'orbit'`: no scene open, a self-posed scene, an Interact grab that missed, and (by omission) everything else. The Interact one is the one that mattered — a left press that missed a body became a camera orbit, so the most delicate action in the studio, reaching for a swinging chain link, was also the action most likely to fling the view.

The replacement is a new `'inert'` gesture rather than `'none'`, and the difference is not cosmetic. `pointermove` returns early on `'none'`, so `moved` never becomes `true`, so `pointerup` sees a clean click — and on the ball drop a clean click spawns a ball. A left drag that grabbed nothing would have dropped a ball on release. `'inert'` owns the drag, sets `moved`, and does nothing with it.

## The water's only visible channel is its silhouette

Riverfall's complaint ("some movement, but too peaceful — doesn't feel like a waterfall") was measured before it was theorised. Two throwaway Playwright drivers: one framing the fall at `viewHeight` 26, one diffing consecutive `.stage` screenshots 200 ms apart in-page.

Baseline: **0.75–1.6% of pixels change per 200 ms**, mean channel delta under 0.6/255.

The reconstruction's whole visible output is a per-tile displacement along the tile normal (`normalExcursion` `[0.03, 0.44]` world units) plus a lean bounded at 0.35 rad. Cranked to `[0.03, 1.8]` with tilt gain 8 → 26 and the advected wavelength 20 → 6, the number moved only to **1.8–2.7%**, and the screenshot says why: the river's upstream surface visibly bulges past the bank, because that is a *silhouette* against green, while the waterfall curtain stays a perfectly flat slab. The 20 fall cells are 5 wide by 4 tall with normals facing the camera, so their excursion moves them toward and away from the viewer; every tile is the same flat colour at 0.62 opacity, and the scene declares zero lights. Displacement inside the sheet has nothing to shade it and nothing to occlude.

The consequence for whoever picks this up: **more displacement will not fix Riverfall.** The fix has to introduce contrast — differently coloured geometry moving across the water, posed live from the solver, in the way `RIVERFALL_FOAM_PLACEMENTS_V1` (currently an empty array) was presumably once meant to. The 576 PBF particles do travel the whole loop and are already mapped to world space every step in `RiverfallLiveSurfaceV1.#captureWitnesses`; they are simply not drawn.

One trap for that work, found the slow way: every field of `createRiverfallFluidConfigV1` — presentation included — is hashed into `canonicalInputJson` and compared against the committed warm state at construction, so changing a display gain makes the scene throw on open until `UPDATE_RIVERFALL_FLUID_REPLAY=1` regenerates both generated files. For a display-only experiment, comment out the `canonicalInputJson` comparison in `riverfall-warm-state.ts` instead; the solver inputs are unchanged and the warm state stays valid.

## A visual gate that was passing on mill phase, not on the thing it measures

`model-studio-windmill-assets.spec.ts`'s eight "Review failure: X is visible in the composed scene" proofs mount the canonical windmill scene, capture two cameras, dispose, mount a static V3 relocation variant, capture the same two cameras, and require one of the two comparisons to clear `minimumRelocationChangedPixelFraction` (0.012) and `minimumChangedChannelDelta` (4).

Both mounts call `setSceneAnimation(false)`, which before this session stopped only the scene clock. Both mills therefore kept turning, at unrelated phases. Unifying the switch stopped the variant's mill and left the canonical's running, which made the comparison swing enough to fail two or three of the eight at random.

Holding both sides still made it deterministic — identical numbers across repeated runs — and turned four of the eight red:

| variant | front | rear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| anvil off grounded head datum | 0.000171 | 0 |
| wheat sack off queue rule | 0.001183 | 0 |
| flour level outside bin | 0.003284 | 0 |
| flour bin off anvil face | 0.011677 | 0 |

Against the running-mill numbers from the same run, where the *rear* view returned 0.024–0.028 for **every** variant including ones whose relocation is invisible from behind. A per-variant proof returning the same number for every variant is measuring something the variants have in common, and what they have in common is that the canonical mill had turned and the static variant had not.

The floor itself inherits the problem. `c22bd6d` set it at half "a tightest real relocation of 2.44%", and that 2.44% was measured under the same running-mill comparison.

Left as it was. `windmill-purpose-review-browser-support.ts` now turns the variant's simulation back on after its `drawAt(0)`, restoring the comparison the floors were calibrated against, with a comment pointing here. Fixing it properly means framing each relocation with a camera that can see it and recalibrating the floor from still-against-still measurements — a change to that proof, not to a controls change passing through.

## Riverfall's fix was contrast, and every wrong turn on the way there was measurable

The owner's report was "some movement, but too peaceful — I can't see water flowing very much. Doesn't feel like a waterfall." The section above establishes the cause: one flat colour has one visible channel, its outline. What follows is what actually worked, and what did not.

**Bigger waves did not work, and could not.** The first experiment quadrupled `normalExcursion` to `[0.03, 1.8]`, tripled the tilt gain to 26, and cut the wavelength from 20 to 6. Changed pixels went from 0.75–1.6% to 1.8–2.7%, and the render explained why: the *river* visibly bulged past its bank — a silhouette against green grass — while the waterfall curtain stayed exactly as flat as before. Its 20 cells carry normals facing the camera, so their displacement moves them toward and away from the viewer, where a uniform colour renders it invisible.

**The wavelength has no headroom at all.** Tiles sit two units apart, so a wave has to span several of them or neighbours disagree and the sheet reads as noise; `riverfall-flow.test.ts` gates the p95 adjacent-height delta at 0.08. Measured across four regenerations of the pinned fixture: **0.1178 at wavelength 8, 0.0989 at 13, 0.0874 at 16**, against roughly 0.077 at the 20 it already had. A shorter wave is not available at this tile pitch, and the comment in the config now says so with the numbers. What is available is phase speed, which has no such limit and is the half that reads as travel: 5 → 12.

**The scene had no light.** `surfaceTilt` exists so that "a light can shade a passing wave" — its own doc comment — and Riverfall declared zero lights, so the entire channel resolved to nothing. Turning the studio's lighting on with no lights in the scene measured 7–11% but rendered the canyon nearly black, because ambient was all there was. Three point lights (key over the lip, fill over the pond, fill over the upstream river) is what makes the tilt gain worth raising.

**Foam is what carries it in the unlit look**, which is the default (`DEFAULT_VIEW_PREFS.lit` is `false`), and shading cannot help there at all. 96 flecks, each riding one of the 576 solved particles. Two tunings were wrong first and are recorded at their constants: a single 0.7-unit voxel rendered as a floating white crate and a row of them across the plunge as a shelf, so a fleck is now a seven-voxel three-axis clump about 0.66 units across; and a 1.4-unit clearance visibly hovered, where the geometry needs 0.9 (a particle sits on the domain centreline, 0.45 outside the drawn surface; a tile reaches 0.05 + 0.5 + up to 0.44 past that plane; a fleck reaches 0.33 back).

**Pool sizes are supply floors, not taste.** A fleck whose particle leaves its band adopts a newly arrived one, which only works if the band always holds more particles than the pool. Measured over a minute of solved play: the curtain-and-plunge band never dropped below 79 and the drawn reach never below 511, so the pools are 32 and 64. `riverfall-spray.test.ts` re-runs that minute and fails with the frame number if any fleck is ever left unassigned — the alternative is a white cube hanging still in the air, which nothing else would catch.

Two smaller things fell out. The catalog's diversity analyzer made the first single-voxel fleck the **nearest neighbour of the windmill anvil**, which says nothing about either model and everything about a degenerate shape; the clump fixed it. And `riverfall-spray.test.ts` first claimed the curtain pool travels furthest, which is false — 1.80 world units per second against the stream pool's 4.46, because the curtain pool is mostly the plunge pool where water arrives and slows, while the stream pool is mostly the river running at its inlet speed. The fall is the fastest place in the scene and the emptiest, holding about 7 particles at a time; those are the same fact.
