## The suspension cart: joint limits and motors under load, and what a wheel taught on the way

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-26

**Action:** Added prismatic joints, declared travel limits, velocity and position motors, station-level contact policies, scenario-owned action timelines, and joint-lock subtraction to the playground's shared vocabulary; built the suspension cart (`studio:scene:physics-cart`) as the second whole machine on them; unified both solver lanes' joint construction in one builder; fixed the headless twin's stale copy of the 2026-08-13 jointed-bodies defect; and made the debug overlay draw a primitive collider's true shape.

### The capability, and where it lives

`physics-joint-build.ts` is the one place a `PlaygroundJointV1` becomes a Rapier joint, for the live studio lane and the headless twin alike. Both lanes used to carry their own copy of the same ternary chain, and limits plus motors made two hand-maintained constructions untenable — a limit present in one lane and absent in the other is two different machines. Limits apply after creation through `setLimits` — the declarative `JointData` path looks equivalent and is not: `intoRaw()` consumes it for prismatic joints only, which the review round below caught — and motors configure after creation on revolute and prismatic joints only, at most one per joint because Rapier carries one motor per axis. `motor-velocity` is a new scripted action — a retarget of an existing declared drive, refused for a joint that declares none — and `lockJoints` is a new scenario field that builds named joints as welds, the mechanism's own subtraction evidence.

Stations can now declare a `contactPolicy`, which the profile generator passes to the live lane and the twin applies at build with the same refusal the live lane already had: a policy alongside spawn-only bodies is refused outright, because a policy must bind every collider it governs. An omitted body takes its pairs with it, the same way a joint touching an omitted body is dropped.

### What the cart measured

The machine: wood chassis, four carriers on vertical prismatic joints (position-motor springs, ±0.25 m declared stops), four driven wheels on revolute axles, unfastened stone cargo, a raised road with five potholes and a half-meter end ledge, thirty declared contact pairs. Fifteen bodies, eight joints; parked, the whole machine sleeps on its brakes (`cart-hold` gates the sleep), and the panel readout during visual inspection showed exactly five contacts — four wheels on the road and the cargo on the deck.

**A faceted wheel at rest on its own flat is a chock.** The first road had quarter-meter ridges, and the cart could not cross them: the d5 wheel's exact voxel colliders present a 0.75 m bottom facet, and at ten times the drive torque the cart wheelied — rear springs slammed to their stops at 7.6° nose-up — while the wheel's spin stayed at zero. At 1.9 m/s the front axle hopped the step and the machine wedged when the rear met the next face. Two consequences shipped: wheels carry `collider: 'cylinder-z'`, the second stated primitive simplification after the rolling station's ideal ball (drawn faceted, simulated round — the readout's 23.6 mass is the cylinder's, not the 25.2 the voxels would weigh), and the road's ridges became potholes, because a 0.625 m wheel bridging a 0.5 m slot dips 0.052 m with an always-rolling exit. The scenery followed the physics — and so did the drawn wheel: the full disc's shoulder corners reach 0.729 m against the 0.625 m tread, 0.104 m of drawn wood inside the drawn road every turn, so the shipped disc is a 13-cell plus whose farthest corner reaches 0.637 m.

**Motor gains act on the joint's reduced mass, not the body's.** Spring stiffness 400 — nominally 3.2 Hz — sagged 0.20 of the 0.25 m travel and rode its own stops; 2000 holds 0.042 m. Drive factor 30 never reached cruise inside the road; 120 spins up in about two seconds. Every constant says it was calibrated by measurement.

**An impulse-based limit yields 0.045 m before holding.** The −900 slam catches at 0.295 m against the declared 0.25; stripped of limits the same slam runs to a measured 0.424. The check's slop is the measured compliance, a guard asserts the slam genuinely reaches the stops, and the counter-run reads the stripped coordinate directly rather than trusting a failing check to mean what its name says.

**The suspension is worth over fourfold in peak chassis vertical acceleration, in both regimes**: 15.1 m/s² sprung against 71.1 m/s² welded across the full drive — both of those peaks are the ledge landing — and 11.6 against 52.0 windowed to the potholed road alone, with the threefold floor asserted in each window. At cruise the locked cart keeps its cargo too, so the ride claim rests on those gaps, not a staged spill — recorded in the scene's ledger honesty rather than overclaimed.

### The instruments that were wrong, and one lane that kept a fixed defect

`crossed-plane` hard-coded negative travel; its own comment promised a direction field to "a future positive-travel station", and the cart is that station — it failed a run that ended at x 17.1 against a 9.5 threshold as "never got past". `playgroundLowestPointV1` judged the smooth-tread wheel by its drawn voxel corners, reporting a phantom 0.104 m burial whenever a facet corner pointed down (√(0.375² + 0.625²) − 0.625 — the arithmetic matched the report to four decimals). Both fixed at the instrument, not the tolerance. The debug overlay drew the voxel boxes for a cylinder body — the overlay's job is the simulated shape, so it now draws the round tread as two sixteen-gon rims.

The twin's `#jointedBodies` was still add-only. The 2026-08-13 session fixed exactly this in the live lane — bearing friction outliving a detached joint — and the fixture lane kept the copy. A released body would have paid 0.8 of bearing damping forever; the twin now recomputes on detach and remove, and a test pins 0.82 held → 0.02 free. The lesson recorded from it: a fix applied to one of two parallel lanes is half a fix, and the shared builder is the systemic half.

### The review round, and what the second pass of critics caught

Codex is still quota-blocked, so two Claude critics with distinct lenses reviewed the uncommitted change — and kept the fleet's streak of every multi-lane review finding something the author missed. The sharpest: **declarative revolute limits are dead in the installed Rapier** — `JointData.intoRaw()` consumes `limitsEnabled` only in its prismatic branch, verified in `rapier.cjs`, while the builder's comment claimed the declarative path verified against the type declarations. Limits now apply post-create through `setLimits`, the runtime path that provably reaches both kinds; the first steering machine would otherwise have shipped an unlimited hinge both lanes agreed on. Second: **the stripped-limit counter-run never measured the overrun** — stripping limits from the station made the travel check fail in its missing-declaration branch before a frame was judged, so a builder that silently dropped limits would have left the counter-run green (proved by neutralization); it reads the stripped coordinate directly now. Third: **the headline ride peaks were the ledge landing, not the road** — the docstring said the exclusion made the number “the road's doing” while both lanes peaked at the drop; the claim held anyway (11.6 vs 52.0 on the road span), and the test now asserts both windows. Fourth: a penetration probe this change had extended still ran its pre-existing trailing else, which **detached the cart's four axles** when handed a motor command — plus a seconds-versus-ticks comparison in the same loop that predates the change; both fixed. Smaller: the stale 0.418 (measured 0.424) quoted in six places and asserted in none; a ledger boundary claiming twenty bodies against its own browser-pinned fifteen; cruise-speed comments saying 3 m/s where the measurement says 1.6–2.2; an ungated “goes to sleep” claim (`cart-hold` now carries `all-asleep-or-slow`); a browser braking assertion that driver latency could pass trivially (reads are step-anchored and speed-bounded now); Rapier's one-motor-per-axis fact (declaring both motors on a joint silently erased the spring — refused at authoring and in the builder now); and the `ends-within` 'apart' polarity plus the travel check's failure branches running in no test — the joint-check family has direct unit coverage now.
