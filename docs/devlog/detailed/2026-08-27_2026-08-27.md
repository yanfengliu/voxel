# Devlog — 2026-08-27

Two halves: the recorded solver-lane divergence resolved by measurement, and a lesson about evidence that was cheaper to relearn than it should have been.

## The KNOWN DIVERGENCE is resolved, and both of its recorded numbers were real

Since 2026-08-13 the profiles module carried a recorded divergence: the headless twin set `softCcdPrediction 0.25` on every dynamic body it built, the live studio lane set it nowhere but the ball-drop rack's spawned balls, and the falling-station drop buried 0.16427 m on screen against 0.00342 m headless. The note refused to pick a side without measurement, and that was right, because both sides turned out to be holding a real fact.

The matrix (every station's key scenario run declared-only and blanket, plus a brick-displacement count on the throw):

- Falling: burial 0.1643 bare against 0.0034 watched — the artifact the studio user actually saw.
- Structures lintel load: the dropped weight buries 0.0728 bare — deep enough to fail its own scenario — against 0.0019 with its declaration shipped.
- Fields: 0.0502 bare against 0.0040 watched — the bare run still passes, because the resting-depth tolerance allows an impact one step of travel, but the pile visibly lands a wrist deep.
- Trebuchet throw: **19 bricks past a quarter meter under the blanket, 12 bare; worst single brick 4.03 m against 5.92 m.** A quarter-meter watch engages the wall early and spreads an impact the discrete step delivers as one launch. Neither count is a law — knockdown is chaotic-regime detail — but the blanket was silently choosing one.
- Ramp, rolling, launcher, cart: differences inside their stated slops either way.

So the watch became what the 60 Hz constant's own comment always said it should be: a per-body declaration. `PlaygroundBodyDefV1.softCcd` flows through the spec seam to both lanes — the live body builder already honoured the plan field; nothing had ever emitted it — and the droppers, field piles, spawn magazines, and structures weights declare it. Impact machines declare nothing and keep the behaviour the studio always showed. `playground-smoke` pins the burial from both sides (0.0034 shipped, and a stripped-declaration counter-run that must exceed 0.1) plus the profile parity (a dropper's plan carries the one shared distance, a brick's and the ball's carry none), and the blanket survives only as `softCcdEverywhere`, the probe lever the counter-run uses.

The migration cost, measured rather than feared: zero. The full playground suite — trebuchet scenarios, rolling races, cart tune, presets — passed unchanged on the first run after the blanket came off. Five weeks of twin measurements had been made in a world the studio never ran, and every one of them held inside its stated slop.

The one broken record found on the way: the Aug 13 note said adding the watch to the live trebuchet took its knockdown from 23 bricks to zero. The twin cannot reproduce anything like that today — its own numbers are 12 against 19, in the *opposite* direction. The mechanism half of the old claim (the watch softens impacts) survives with the right sign in the worst-single-brick number; the "to zero" half is left as what it is, an observation from a configuration that no longer exists.

## Four probes to exonerate a camera, or: a frame nobody looked at is not evidence

The visual sweep after the soft-CCD fix found the trebuchet's `wall-before-impact` and `wall-rubble` shots showing bare floor — no wall, no rubble — and the same frames in the previous session's output, byte-similar. They had shipped twice. The sweep that shipped them had inspected the cart frames, because the cart was the work, and never opened the wall's.

The diagnosis then burned four instrumented probes on a camera that was never broken:

1. Fire-timing probe: the fire works headlessly, ball downrange on schedule.
2. Exact-preamble probe: the fire works after the script's screenshots and overlay toggles too.
3. Pan-overwrite probe: `setViewCentre` readback holds `[0, 2, -32]` across frames — no overwrite.
4. Composition check by hand: at pitch 16° and viewHeight 20, centred 7.5 m past the wall, the 12 m-wide floor renders as a grazing diagonal sliver spanning 72 m of ground, in which a 1.7 m wall is about eight pixels of jagged edge — present, rendered, unrecognisable.

The readout that had looked like proof of an unfired machine — "4 awake" three seconds after the fire button — was the fired machine: arm, crate, sling, and ball are exactly the four bodies still swinging after the throw while 33 bricks and the fixed frame sleep, and the arm's swing-back pose passes within centimetres of its cocked pose.

The shots recompose on the wall itself (pitch 22–24°, viewHeight 9–14, centred on the wall) with waits anchored to the ball's own z rather than blind timers, and the wall now tells its story in three frames: running bond standing, ball punched through the middle courses, rubble field. The falling station gained a landing-instant shot at floor level — the frame where the 0.164 m burial used to live, now showing a cube flush on the floor line with 0.0014 m of contact depth in the readout.

The canon already commands sweeping the whole rendered result; this is what partial compliance costs. The camera was innocent, the composition was the defect, and it survived exactly as long as nobody looked.
