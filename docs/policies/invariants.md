# Invariants in detail (voxel)

Each entry is a rule of the Invariants & boundaries section of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) in full, the owner's laws for scenes and physics among them; AGENTS.md keeps its opening. The rest moved here from AGENTS.md, sentence for sentence, on 2026-10-29.

- Scenes simulate live (owner rule, 2026-07-30): every scene computes its motion in real time, at frame rate, in the browser. This is the whole point of having scenes — they exist to exercise the runtime a real-time game will actually use, and a scene that replays baked poses tests the decoder instead. A pre-recorded trace is never a scene's source of truth; it is at most a determinism fixture a test compares against, and it must never be what the studio plays back for the owner. Every scene is now live: Machine Works, Riverfall, Windmill, and the chain were the four still recorded when this rule landed, and all four were converted — `tools/studio/live-physics-profiles.ts` registers a live profile for each, and `tools/studio/catalog.test.ts` refuses a recorded shelf scene. No new scene may be added on the recorded lane. Judge a live scene by what the renderer sustains: frame budget, draw calls, and resource counts under real simulation, inspected on screen rather than measured from a file.
- Everything solves at 60 Hz (owner rule, 2026-07-31): one lane constant, `LIVE_TIMESTEP_SECONDS_V1`, and every solver lane derives its step from it rather than spelling a literal. 240 Hz was the owner's monitor refresh rate, never a target — no consumer runs that high, and a rate nobody ships is a rate nothing is really tested at. A lane that cannot yet meet it says so where its constant is declared, with the measurement that blocks it, and is delivery work rather than a permanent exception. `tools/studio/solver-rate.test.ts` fails on any lane that drifts off the shared constant.
- Accepted state is tracked separately from presented state: mesh swaps at frame boundaries, picking reads the same presented data as the canvas, capture waits for or reports incomplete presentation. Committed `pickPresented` requires a world whose descriptor carries `chunkProfile` and whose runtime enables `voxelWorkers`; other paths report typed unavailable outcomes (`no-presented-frame`, lost/restoring/failed/disposed).
- Physics flaws are fixed as universal law (owner rule, 2026-07-31): when a simulation behaves impossibly, the repair is a law of this voxel universe, never a patch to the scene that exposed it. State each law as what holds, not what is banned: surfaces resist sliding, rolling contact resists the roll, a bearing resists its shaft, air resists both travel and spin, a collision returns less than it receives, a passive machine only ever spends the energy it started with, and Newton's three laws hold and are measured rather than assumed. Laws live in one module, published as `voxel/physics`, and are applied where rigid bodies are created — every solver lane in this repository, the consumer fixtures included — so no scene here can opt out by forgetting to opt in. A downstream game keeps its own solver and does not inherit them automatically; it borrows them by calling `applyPhysicsLawsToBodyV1` at its own body-creation site, which is the whole reason the module ships free of Three.js, Rapier, and the DOM. Content may tune a law per material and must state the measured effect; content may never exempt itself, and a law that a body can escape by declaring nothing is not a law. Prove each with a counter-run that fails when the law is overridden away, and prove no content escapes with a test that walks every material and every body. When a new law shortens a throw or moves a landing point, move the scenery to follow the physics — bending a machine to protect a number is how a workaround becomes furniture. Retire the scenery that existed only to compensate for the missing law.
- A mechanism declares which of its parts may touch (2026-07-31, commit `be30dd8`). A live physics profile carries a `contactPolicy` naming the permitted body pairs, and every collider not named in one is inert for that mechanism — which is how a shaft passes through the housing that holds it and a cam slips off its follower instead of gripping it. The everything-meets-everything default stays for a chain or a heap, which need it. Declaring the pairs is not a simplification of the mechanism, it is the mechanism, so a contact a machine relies on is named rather than discovered by the solver; `windmill-scene.test.ts` pins that profile's two.
- Placed things are solid (owner rule, 2026-07-30): no two placements may co-exist in the same space — recorded poses included — and no two same-facing surfaces may lie on one rendered plane, because coincident same-facing area is what fights for visibility and always means shared volume. Flush back-to-back contact is the only legal coincidence; recorded physics may dent a contact only by its numerical slop; where two grains must nearly meet, keep them apart by an authored clearance off every involved lattice (the Machine Works still-settle is the pattern). Enforce at content boundaries — `sceneOverlapsV1`, `sceneSurfaceFightsV1`, the studio's live conflict line, and the fixtures' generation-time penetration budgets — and fix geometry at its source; never hide a fight with renderer depth tricks.
